Abstract & Diagnostic Problem Chain

How Far Can Dispersion Misfit Reduction Be Trusted?

“Without shear-wave velocity (Vs) ground truth on field arrays, how far can held-out dispersion misfit reduction be trusted as evidence of subsurface velocity improvement?”

Pretrained neural networks invert Rayleigh-wave dispersion curves orders of magnitude faster than classical global optimization, but their accuracy degrades when target arrays shift in period sampling, sedimentary velocity priors, or lithospheric structure. Two obstacles confound label-free test-time adaptation (TTA): coarse root bracketing in matrix-propagator forward solvers can misidentify modal branches on low-velocity zones, and ill-posed dispersion kernels exhibit semi-convergence near observational noise levels. Across ten TTA pipelines, two Transformer backbones (OpenSWI-Transformer and DispFormer), six synthetic domains, and two field arrays (LongBeach and the China Seismological Reference Model, CSRM), SWI-TTA establishes converged forward-solver auditing, quantifies noise-floor-normalized regime signatures, and implements an open-source, CPU-reproducible four-step diagnostic protocol.

Section 3 · Forward-Solver Convergence Audit

Hidden Pitfall: Coarse Root Bracketing Skips Mode 0 on Shallow LVZs

Under disba’s default search step (Δc = 5.0×10−3 km/s), quasi-degenerate osculation between Mode 0 and Mode 1 in shallow profiles containing sub-resolvable low-velocity zones triggers catastrophic artificial misfit tails. Refining solely to Δc = 2.5×10−5 km/s recovers Mode 0 without changing a single velocity layer.

Sections 4 – 6.1 · Benchmark Divergence & Semi-Convergence

Noise-Floor Regimes Govern Adaptation Across Backbones

Once forward scoring is converged, stratifying stations by initial exact misfit normalized by the noise floor (r0GT = m0 / σGT) separates below-floor null-space noise overfitting, intermediate-shift structural recovery, and deep extreme-outlier instability across both OpenSWI-Transformer and DispFormer.

Figure 2. Input alignment removes major cross-grid error at zero gradient cost. Subsequent TTA cuts Vs RMSE by −57.79% under strong prior shift (LongBeach-Matched), yet degrades Deep Benchmark RMSE by +21.86% even while held-out dispersion misfit decreases from 1.681% to 1.668%.

Figure 5. Architecture-independent three-regime transitions across OpenSWI-Transformer and DispFormer, including consistent below-floor degradation in [0, 1) on clean retrained seeds (+13.25% OpenSWI, +15.24% DispFormer).

Figure 6. Scaling observational noise from 0.5× to 4.0× on identical profiles flips 50-step refinement monotonically from −9.56% gain to +46.93% harm while unlabeled classical calibration σ̂D tracks true noise linearly (0.929–0.953×) and Holdout ES improves every noise level.

Figure 7. Why single-threshold Morozov discrepancy stopping (mi ≤ ασ̂D) fails simultaneous shallow and deep validation (0/4): shallow transition-band (1 < r0 ≤ 2) overshoot demands α ≥ 2.0, whereas deep r0 > 5 outliers inject >10 km crustal oscillations.

Section 6.2 · Field Array Consistency

Contrasting Field Regimes: LongBeach Nodal Array vs. CSRM Grid

On LongBeach Report (3,705 stations; operational r0 ≈ 6.3×), adaptation recovers the NW–SE Newport–Inglewood fault-zone ridge and lowers held-out misfit from 3.75% to 0.82%. On CSRM Report (8,842 grid points; r0 ≈ 0.91×), zero-shot already sits near the noise floor, while unconstrained per-station updates inject mid-crustal speckle (e.g., Tibetan Plateau point #10067).

Figure 8. Horizontal Vs slices across LongBeach Report (0.12–0.48 km): adapted pipelines correct zero-shot sedimentary over-prediction (rref = 0.551 → 0.713 under protocol; 0.718 single-stage) and delineate the Newport–Inglewood fault-zone high-velocity contrast.

Section 6.2 & Table S3 · Reproducible Operational Workflow

Four-Step Operational Protocol & Closed-Loop 14-Cohort Audit
